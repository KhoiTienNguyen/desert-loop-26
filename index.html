AREA 01

South Rim viewpoints and canyon trails

Grand Canyon

Sun · Oct 25 — Tue · Oct 27

Begin slowly. The first evening is for arrival; the full day is for one real walk and a sequence of rim viewpoints; the eastbound departure is its own scenic day.

Sun · Oct 25LV-GC

Transfer day

Las Vegas to the South Rim

Las Vegas → Mather Campground · 275–285 mi · 4.5–5 hr before stops

TYPICAL TEMPERATURES
Las VegasMIN 13° MAX 25°C
South RimMIN -1° MAX 16°C
DAY PLAN

Treat every stop below as optional. Rental pickup, groceries and the vehicle inspection matter more than forcing sunset.

#01TOP PRIORITY

rim viewpoint · 30–60 min

Mather Point

The cleanest first encounter with the canyon: broad terraces, branching side canyons and a short paved approach from the visitor center.

Why it is worth the stop. Go only if you reach the rim with safe daylight; otherwise save the reveal for morning.

Official planning information ↗
#02smart sunset fallback

viewpoint and geology exhibit · 45–75 min

Yavapai Point & Geology Museum

A close, legible view into the inner canyon paired with displays that make the rock layers easier to read.

Why it is worth the stop. Near Mather and easier to abandon quickly if pickup runs late.

Official planning information ↗
#03only with time

historic-road town · 20–45 min

Seligman Route 66

A neon-and-diner fragment of old Route 66 that fits the trip's retro road-movie mood.

Why it is worth the stop. A character stop, not a reason to arrive at camp after dark.

Official planning information ↗
Mon · Oct 26GC-RIM

Full park day

A full South Rim day

Mather base · shuttle and walking day

TYPICAL TEMPERATURES
South RimMIN -2° MAX 16°C
DAY PLAN

Pick either an into-the-canyon hike or a longer Hermit Road walk as the anchor. Do not combine both ambitious versions.

#01TOP PRIORITY

steep canyon hike · 2–3 hr

South Kaibab to Ooh Aah Point

A short but serious descent to an exposed saddle with one of the quickest immersive views below the rim.

Why it is worth the stop. Best visual return for a half-day, but the climb back is steep and there is no water on trail.

Official planning information ↗
#02best no-descent plan

shuttle viewpoints and rim walk · 2–4 hr

Hopi Point + Rim Trail

Ride the Red Route, then walk selected rim sections between overlooks for changing Colorado River views.

Why it is worth the stop. Hopi gives a wide west-facing panorama; the shuttle lets the group tune distance and weather exposure.

Official planning information ↗
#03scenic extension

quiet rim walk · 1.5–2.5 hr

Pima Point to Hermits Rest

A quieter western segment where the river can sometimes be heard far below before ending at the historic rest house.

Why it is worth the stop. Ideal add-on after a shorter Hermit Road circuit.

Official planning information ↗
#04easy alternative

paved interpretive rim walk · 1–2 hr

Trail of Time

A geology walk between Yavapai and Grand Canyon Village that turns the canyon's age into a walkable timeline.

Why it is worth the stop. The best low-exertion option when wind, ice or tired legs rule out a descent.

Official planning information ↗
#05swap for South Kaibab

steep canyon hike · 3–5 hr

Bright Angel to 1.5-Mile Resthouse

A historic trail dropping through the upper rock layers with more shade than South Kaibab.

Why it is worth the stop. Choose this instead of Ooh Aah—not in addition—and turn back early if the upper trail is icy.

Official planning information ↗
Tue · Oct 27GC-MV

Scenic transfer

Desert View Road to Monument Valley

Mather → Monument Valley · 175–185 mi · clock moves +1 hr

TYPICAL TEMPERATURES
South RimMIN -1° MAX 15°C
Monument ValleyMIN 6° MAX 20°C
DAY PLAN

The east-rim road has more worthy stops than the schedule can hold. Grandview, Navajo Point and Desert View are the clean first cut. Lipan Point is excluded because it is scheduled closed during the trip.

#01TOP PRIORITY

historic tower and river viewpoint · 60–90 min

Desert View Watchtower

Mary Colter's stone tower stands above one of the rim's clearest views of the Colorado River bending west.

Why it is worth the stop. It is both the route's strongest cultural stop and the natural finale before leaving the park.

Official planning information ↗
#02best east-rim panorama

panoramic viewpoint · 20–40 min

Grandview Point

A high promontory with long views both east and west and visible bends of the river.

Why it is worth the stop. The most rewarding quick stop early on Desert View Drive.

Official planning information ↗
#03best watchtower composition

high rim viewpoint · 15–30 min

Navajo Point

The South Rim's highest developed overlook frames the Watchtower against layers of canyon and sky.

Why it is worth the stop. A distinct photograph and perspective only minutes from Desert View.

Official planning information ↗
#04culture option

ancestral Pueblo site · 30–45 min

Tusayan Pueblo

A short self-guided path circles the remains of a small Ancestral Puebloan village.

Why it is worth the stop. Adds human history to a day otherwise dominated by geology; scheduled open through October 31.

Official planning information ↗
#05arrival prize

tribal-park panorama · 45–75 min

Monument Valley overlook at sunset

The classic view from the visitor area places the Mittens and Merrick Butte in an immense open desert.

Why it is worth the stop. Protect this only if the time-zone change, driving and campsite setup still leave safe daylight.

Official planning information ↗
AREA 02

Navajo Tribal Park and desert roads

Monument Valley

Wed · Oct 28 — Wed · Oct 28

This is not an RV drive-through. Let a Navajo guide carry the morning, or walk the one self-guided loop before the transfer north.

Wed · Oct 28MV-MOAB

Half-day experience + transfer

Monument Valley morning, Moab by dusk

Monument Valley → Ken's Lake · 150–160 mi · 3–3.5 hr before stops

TYPICAL TEMPERATURES
Monument ValleyMIN 6° MAX 20°C
DAY PLAN

Choose the guided valley tour or the full Wildcat loop. The campervan is prohibited on Valley Drive; do not improvise around that rule.

#01TOP PRIORITY

guided cultural landscape tour · 2.5–3.5 hr

Navajo-guided Valley Drive tour

A local guide takes the group among the monumental buttes and into areas that self-guided visitors cannot access.

Why it is worth the stop. It solves the campervan prohibition and adds Navajo interpretation rather than reducing the valley to a photo stop.

Official planning information ↗
#02best independent alternative

self-guided desert hike · 2–3 hr

Wildcat Trail

The park's self-guided loop circles West Mitten through open desert with changing views of the three iconic buttes.

Why it is worth the stop. A strong substitute if tour timing or cost does not work; start early and carry water.

Official planning information ↗
#03quick northbound stop

roadside long view · 15–25 min

Forrest Gump Point

A straight ribbon of US-163 runs toward the Monument Valley skyline from the north.

Why it is worth the stop. It is naturally on the route toward Moab, but use only a safe pullout and never stand in traffic.

Official planning information ↗
#04easy transfer break

roadside sandstone arch · 15–35 min

Wilson Arch

A large arch rises directly beside US-191 south of Moab, with a short steep scramble for a closer look.

Why it is worth the stop. A zero-detour taste of Utah sandstone after the long transfer.

Official planning information ↗
#05only if early

river corridor and logistics stop · 30–60 min

Kane Creek / Moab supply reset

A short sunset drive beside the Colorado can add river-and-cliff scenery before camp.

Why it is worth the stop. Supplies and water come first; this is a mood-setting bonus only.

Official planning information ↗
AREA 03

Arches, Canyonlands and the Colorado Plateau

Moab Country

Thu · Oct 29 — Fri · Oct 30

Two full days, two different scales: walk among arches first, then move to the high mesa where the Colorado and Green Rivers carve the land below.

Thu · Oct 29ARCH

Full park day

Arches: fins, windows and one perfect span

Ken's Lake → Arches scenic road → Ken's Lake

TYPICAL TEMPERATURES
MoabMIN 5° MAX 22°C
DAY PLAN

The recommended sequence is Devils Garden early, Windows midday, Delicate Arch late. If the group prefers less hiking, replace Delicate Arch with the viewpoint—not with another long trail.

#01TOP PRIORITY

iconic sandstone arch hike · 2.5–3.5 hr

Delicate Arch

A three-mile round trip crosses open slickrock to Utah's freestanding emblem above a natural sandstone bowl.

Why it is worth the stop. The strongest single image and experience in Arches; late light is beautiful, but return requires darkness-ready planning.

Official planning information ↗
#02best morning hike

easy fin-and-arch hike · 1.5–2.5 hr

Devils Garden to Landscape Arch

A relatively easy 1.9-mile round trip threads through sandstone fins to North America's longest arch span.

Why it is worth the stop. Start here before parking fills; the light also favors Landscape Arch in the morning.

Official planning information ↗
#03highest variety per mile

short linked arch walks · 1.5–2.5 hr

Windows + Double Arch

North Window, Turret Arch and massive Double Arch cluster in one compact, visually dense district.

Why it is worth the stop. The best all-abilities core stop and an easy place to adjust the day's walking load.

Official planning information ↗
#04cinematic opener

sandstone canyon walk · 45–90 min

Park Avenue

A corridor of monoliths and sheer walls feels more like a stone city street than an arch trail.

Why it is worth the stop. Walk one-way with a pickup, or use the overlook as a quick morning scene-setter.

Official planning information ↗
#05swap for Delicate Arch

difficult slickrock hike · 3–5 hr

Double O Arch

Beyond Landscape Arch, the trail climbs narrow fins and exposed sandstone to a double opening.

Why it is worth the stop. Choose it for scrambling and solitude, but not on wet or icy rock and not in addition to every other long hike.

Official planning information ↗
#06easy filler

roadside geologic formation · 15–30 min

Balanced Rock

A huge caprock appears impossibly poised above its eroded pedestal beside the scenic road.

Why it is worth the stop. A genuinely worthwhile quick stop between the Windows and Delicate Arch areas.

Official planning information ↗
Fri · Oct 30CANY-DHP

Full scenic circuit

Island in the Sky to Dead Horse Point

Ken's Lake → Island in the Sky → Kayenta Campground

TYPICAL TEMPERATURES
Island in the SkyMIN 2° MAX 17°C
DAY PLAN

Mesa Arch, Grand View and Dead Horse sunset are the core. Add either Upheaval Dome or a longer rim walk—not both unless the group starts very early.

#01TOP PRIORITY

mesa-top panorama and rim walk · 45–120 min

Grand View Point

The road ends on a cliff above a maze of canyons, with a one-mile rim extension for progressively wider views.

Why it is worth the stop. The most complete explanation of Island in the Sky's enormous scale.

Official planning information ↗
#02essential short walk

short arch hike · 45–75 min

Mesa Arch

A 0.5-mile loop reaches a cliff-edge arch framing Buck Canyon and the La Sal Mountains.

Why it is worth the stop. A famous sunrise subject, but its composition works all day and crowds are usually gentler later.

Official planning information ↗
#03sunset anchor

Colorado River overlook · 45–90 min

Dead Horse Point Overlook

A short paved path reaches the park's defining view, 2,000 feet above a sweeping Colorado River bend.

Why it is worth the stop. The light and overnight location make this the day's natural finale.

Official planning information ↗
#04distinct second river view

river-canyon viewpoint · 20–40 min

Green River Overlook

An open western overlook looks across the Green River's canyon toward the Maze and distant plateaus.

Why it is worth the stop. A different watershed and softer composition than Grand View.

Official planning information ↗
#05science-minded swap

geologic crater hike · 1.5–2.5 hr

Upheaval Dome

A short steep trail reaches overlooks into a strange circular structure of tilted, colorful rock.

Why it is worth the stop. Pick this when unusual geology matters more than another rim panorama.

Official planning information ↗
#06solitude add-on

quiet slickrock rim hike · 2–3 hr

Bighorn Overlook

A cairned route from near Kayenta reaches an isolated overlook whose views rival the main point.

Why it is worth the stop. A strong campsite-adjacent option if the group arrives well before sunset.

Official planning information ↗
AREA 04

Fruita, the Waterpocket Fold and Highway 12

Capitol Reef + Highway 12

Sat · Oct 31 — Sun · Nov 1

Fruita brings orchards, petroglyphs and pioneer history into the red-rock landscape. Highway 12 then climbs over Boulder Mountain and crosses the exposed Hogsback on the way to Bryce.

Sat · Oct 31DHP-CARE

Transfer + half park day

Dead Horse dawn, Capitol Reef afternoon

Kayenta → Fruita · 155–165 mi · 3–3.5 hr

TYPICAL TEMPERATURES
FruitaMIN -1° MAX 14°C
DAY PLAN

Hickman Bridge plus the Fruita core is the realistic first plan. Scenic Drive or Grand Wash is a swap if arrival is early; road access depends on weather and vehicle length.

#01TOP PRIORITY

natural bridge hike · 1.5–2.5 hr

Hickman Bridge

A moderate two-mile round trip climbs from the Fremont River to a 133-foot natural bridge and broad canyon views.

Why it is worth the stop. It delivers the park's signature geology without consuming the whole transfer day.

Official planning information ↗
#02essential atmosphere

orchards and pioneer landscape · 45–90 min

Fruita Historic District

Cottonwoods, orchards, barns and red cliffs create the park's softest and most nostalgic landscape.

Why it is worth the stop. It is beside camp and gives Capitol Reef a human scale distinct from the other parks.

Official planning information ↗
#03essential culture stop

Fremont culture rock art · 30–45 min

Petroglyph panels

Boardwalks along SR 24 lead to Fremont Culture figures pecked into the cliff face roughly 700–1,000 years ago.

Why it is worth the stop. A brief, high-value stop that should not be displaced by another viewpoint.

Official planning information ↗
#04flat-hike swap

easy canyon hike · 1.5–3 hr

Grand Wash narrows

A broad wash tightens between high sandstone walls on a mostly level route accessible from SR 24.

Why it is worth the stop. The best low-elevation substitute if Hickman feels too steep or the van cannot use the spur road.

Official planning information ↗
#05vehicle-dependent option

paved and dirt scenic drive · 1.5–2.5 hr

Scenic Drive + Grand Wash spur

The paved drive follows the Waterpocket Fold; the dirt spur enters a canyon only when open and compatible.

Why it is worth the stop. A relaxed option after the transfer, but never worth forcing in mud, ice or a vehicle over the governing limit.

Official planning information ↗
#06late-light fallback

short panorama walks · 45–75 min

Goosenecks + Sunset Point

Two small trails near the park's west side reveal Sulphur Creek's bends and a wide evening panorama.

Why it is worth the stop. The simplest strong finish when the longer hikes no longer fit.

Official planning information ↗
Sun · Nov 1HWY12-BRCA

Scenic road day

Highway 12 to Bryce Canyon

Fruita → Bryce · 110–120 mi · 3+ hr without stops · clocks fall back

TYPICAL TEMPERATURES
FruitaMIN -1° MAX 14°C
BryceMIN -5° MAX 7°C
DAY PLAN

The road itself is the attraction. Stop at two or three major overlooks and protect Bryce late light. Lower Calf Creek Falls is a full-day trade, not a casual add-on.

#01TOP PRIORITY

All-American scenic road · 3–5 hr with stops

Highway 12 drive: Boulder Mountain to the Hogsback

The road drops from pine-and-aspen high country into slickrock, then balances along a narrow ridge above deep canyons.

Why it is worth the stop. Few drives show so many landscape transitions in one continuous line.

Official planning information ↗
#02best desert pullout

slickrock panorama · 15–30 min

Head of the Rocks Overlook

A roadside overlook opens across cream-and-orange Escalante slickrock toward Boulder Mountain and the Henry Mountains.

Why it is worth the stop. The cleanest quick visual summary of Grand Staircase–Escalante.

Official planning information ↗
#03best mountain views

high-elevation viewpoints · 15–30 min each

Homestead / Larb Hollow Overlooks

Boulder Mountain pullouts look east across the Waterpocket Fold toward the Henry and Navajo Mountains.

Why it is worth the stop. Distinct from the desert stops and worth choosing before any snow or fog closes the view.

Official planning information ↗
#04weather-proof culture stop

ancestral Pueblo village museum · 45–75 min

Anasazi State Park Museum

A compact museum and excavated village in Boulder interpret one of the region's largest known ancestral communities.

Why it is worth the stop. The strongest indoor swap if exposed overlooks are cold, wet or clouded in.

Official planning information ↗
#05full-day replacement

desert waterfall hike · 4–5 hr

Lower Calf Creek Falls

A six-mile round trip follows a canyon to a 126-foot waterfall and pool beneath mineral-streaked walls.

Why it is worth the stop. Beautiful, but it replaces Bryce afternoon and most scenic pullouts; do it only if the group deliberately accepts that cost.

Official planning information ↗
#06arrival finale

hoodoo amphitheater viewpoint · 45–75 min

Sunset Point, Bryce

The first rim view looks directly onto Thor's Hammer and the dense hoodoos below Sunset Point.

Why it is worth the stop. Protect this if road and weather conditions allow a comfortable arrival before dark.

Official planning information ↗
AREA 05

High-elevation hoodoos and rim trails

Bryce Canyon

Mon · Nov 2 — Mon · Nov 2

A compact, high-elevation chapter. Descend among the hoodoos if the trail is dry; otherwise let the rim and southern overlooks do the work.

Mon · Nov 2BRCA-ZION

Morning park + transfer

Bryce morning, Zion by afternoon

Bryce → Zion · 85–90 mi east route or ~150 mi via I-15

TYPICAL TEMPERATURES
BryceMIN -5° MAX 7°C
DAY PLAN

Queen's Garden/Navajo is the first choice when dry. On snow or ice, stay on the rim and drive the southern viewpoints. Canyon Overlook at Zion fits only with safe east-route access and parking.

#01TOP PRIORITY

hoodoo amphitheater hike · 2–3 hr

Queen's Garden + Navajo combination

A 2.9-mile loop descends from Sunrise Point among hoodoos and climbs the dramatic Two Bridges side of Navajo Loop.

Why it is worth the stop. The park's recommended first-timer hike and the best way to experience hoodoos at human scale.

Official planning information ↗
#02best icy-day plan

easy rim walk · 45–75 min

Sunrise to Sunset Rim Trail

A paved one-mile round trip connects the two central amphitheater viewpoints above Thor's Hammer.

Why it is worth the stop. High visual return with almost no route cost; ideal if cold or trail ice discourages descent.

Official planning information ↗
#03best grandstand view

tiered amphitheater viewpoint · 30–60 min

Inspiration Point

A short uphill series of overlooks reveals the densest section of the Bryce Amphitheater from above.

Why it is worth the stop. The strongest single rim panorama accessible to vehicles over 20 feet.

Official planning information ↗
#04weather-aware road swap

southern scenic-drive overlooks · 1.5–2.5 hr

Natural Bridge + Rainbow Point

The high southern road pairs a red rock arch with long views from the plateau's far end.

Why it is worth the stop. Choose this instead of a canyon hike when roads are clear but foot trails are icy.

Official planning information ↗
#05east-route arrival bonus

short cliffside hike · 1–1.5 hr

Canyon Overlook, Zion

A one-mile round trip ends above lower Zion Canyon with the switchback road curling through the cliffs below.

Why it is worth the stop. A superb bridge between Bryce and Zion, but parking is extremely limited and the vehicle must be legal on the east corridor.

Official planning information ↗
AREA 06

Zion Canyon, West Rim and the Virgin River

Zion

Tue · Nov 3 — Thu · Nov 5

Two full days make room for weather. Pair one ambitious canyon hike with one flexible river day, then leave the return morning nearly empty.

Tue · Nov 3ZION-HIGH

Full park day

West Rim Trail and Zion Canyon views

Zion Canyon shuttle day

TYPICAL TEMPERATURES
Zion CanyonMIN 3° MAX 18°C
DAY PLAN

Scout Lookout is the guaranteed anchor. Angels Landing is an optional permitted extension, never an assumption. Emerald Pools is the gentler swap.

#01TOP PRIORITY

strenuous switchback hike · 3–4 hr

Scout Lookout via West Rim Trail

The paved West Rim Trail climbs through Refrigerator Canyon and Walter's Wiggles to a broad overlook above Zion Canyon.

Why it is worth the stop. It delivers most of the famous elevation and drama without requiring an Angels Landing permit.

Official planning information ↗
#02permit-only extension

permitted exposed ridge hike · +1–2 hr beyond Scout

Angels Landing chain section

A narrow, chain-assisted ridge continues from Scout Lookout to the iconic summit above Zion Canyon.

Why it is worth the stop. A rare standout for hikers comfortable with extreme exposure; apply in the day-before lottery and skip in bad weather.

Official planning information ↗
#03best gentler alternative

moderate canyon-side loop · 2–3 hr

Kayenta + Emerald Pools

A ledge trail links the Grotto to alcoves, pools and waterfalls opposite the main canyon walls.

Why it is worth the stop. A flexible half-day with easy shuttle access; water flow is seasonal and swimming is prohibited.

Official planning information ↗
#04easy evening finish

paved river walk · 60–90 min

Pa'rus Trail at sunset

A nearly level path follows the Virgin River to classic evening views of the Watchman.

Why it is worth the stop. Start from camp or the visitor center and finish the day without another shuttle commitment.

Official planning information ↗
#05shuttle-free swap

moderate lower-canyon hike · 2–3 hr

Watchman Trail

A 3.3-mile loop climbs above the visitor center to views of the Towers of the Virgin, Springdale and lower canyon.

Why it is worth the stop. The best self-starting alternative if shuttle crowds or timing undermine the main-canyon plan.

Official planning information ↗
Wed · Nov 4ZION-RIVER

Full flexible day

The Narrows, with dry-land alternatives

Temple of Sinawava and Zion Canyon

TYPICAL TEMPERATURES
Zion CanyonMIN 3° MAX 18°C
DAY PLAN

The Narrows is entirely condition-dependent in November. If water, flash-flood, cold or cyanobacteria guidance is wrong, use Riverside Walk plus the dry-land alternatives without regret.

#01TOP PRIORITY

river hike in slot canyon · 3–6 hr

The Narrows, bottom-up

Hike directly in the Virgin River between sheer walls, turning around whenever cold, depth or fatigue says so.

Why it is worth the stop. Zion's most singular experience, but not automatically the right choice in cold fall water.

Official planning information ↗
#02all-weather canyon core

easy canyon-end walk · 1.5–2 hr

Riverside Walk

A paved 2.2-mile round trip follows the Virgin River beneath hanging gardens to the start of the Narrows.

Why it is worth the stop. Worth doing even if nobody enters the water, and easy to combine with another shorter trail.

Official planning information ↗
#03dry-land replacement

quiet mesa-and-canyon hike · 3–4 hr

Sand Bench Trail

A sandy route climbs onto a massive ancient landslide beneath the Sentinel with unusual angles on Zion Canyon.

Why it is worth the stop. A substantial, often quieter substitute when the Narrows is unsafe or unappealing.

Official planning information ↗
#04western-district swap

short ridge hike · 1–1.5 hr plus drive

Timber Creek Overlook, Kolob Canyons

A short ridge walk at Zion's northwest corner looks across red-finger canyons toward the Pine Valley Mountains.

Why it is worth the stop. A dramatically different Zion scene, but it replaces most of a main-canyon day because of the drive.

Official planning information ↗
#05bad-weather reset

cultural stop and viewpoint · 30–60 min

Human History Museum patio

Compact exhibits connect people to the canyon, while the patio holds one of the easiest Towers of the Virgin views.

Why it is worth the stop. A useful pause when weather or tired legs shorten the day.

Official planning information ↗
Thu · Nov 5ZION-LV

Return day

Zion morning and the drive to Las Vegas

Zion → Las Vegas · ~165 mi · 2.5–3 hr · gain 1 clock hr

TYPICAL TEMPERATURES
ZionMIN 3° MAX 18°C
Las VegasMIN 11° MAX 22°C
DAY PLAN

The rental return cutoff is the fixed point. Choose at most one short morning walk or roadside break; fuel, waste, cleaning and inspection are part of the drive time.

#01TOP PRIORITY

easy river walk · 30–60 min

Pa'rus Trail dawn walk

A brief out-and-back from the visitor-center area catches soft light on lower Zion without moving the campervan far.

Why it is worth the stop. The cleanest final experience because it is easy to shorten instantly.

Official planning information ↗
#02quick photo option

roadside viewpoints · 15–30 min

Canyon Junction / Towers of the Virgin views

Classic lower-canyon walls rise directly above the road and museum area.

Why it is worth the stop. A visual farewell with almost no schedule risk; stop only in legal parking.

Official planning information ↗
#03only with late return

state-park road and short walks · 2.5–4 hr detour

Valley of Fire scenic drive

Red sandstone fins, Elephant Rock and the White Domes road form a vivid final Nevada chapter.

Why it is worth the stop. A genuine attraction, but it is a full detour that must be approved by the rental-return schedule—not squeezed in casually.
